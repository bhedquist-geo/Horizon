# Evaluation goal

Evaluate how useful this item is to a university geospatial instructor and researcher who is building GeoAI and geospatial data science skills, redesigning GIS and remote sensing courses, and following current Earth-observation research. Score the item itself, not the reputation of its source. Score 0 for any item that is not written in English.

# Relevance gate

Apply this first. An item must have geospatial, Earth-observation, remote sensing, mapping, or GIS subject matter, or be a release of a core geospatial tool. An item without that subject matter scores 0-2 however strong the work is. Examples that must score 0-2: medical or biological imaging, general computer vision or language-model work with no geospatial data or application, robotics or driving work that does not use geographic or overhead data, and general AI news.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Exceptional.** A significant advance or resource for GeoAI or geospatial data science, such as a new open model, dataset, benchmark, or tool with clear evidence on real geospatial data, that a practitioner could adopt or teach from right away.
- **7-8: High value.** Substantive work with a clear method, open code or data, and credible evaluation on real geospatial or Earth-observation data, or a release that adds meaningful new capability to a core tool.
- **5-6: Useful.** Competent and on topic but incremental, narrow, lightly evidenced, or mainly relevant to a specialized subfield.
- **3-4: Marginal.** Loosely connected to geospatial work, mostly generic AI content, a routine release, or promotional material with little technical substance.
- **0-2: Noise.** Off topic, failing the relevance gate, purely promotional, misleading, too incomplete to evaluate, or not in English.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward reproducibility (open code, open data, documented methods), evaluation on real geospatial data with sensible baselines, honest discussion of limitations, and material that can be turned into a course exercise or training project. Distinguish measured results from vendor claims. Do not reward hype, brand reputation, or novelty without evidence. Treat routine bug-fix releases as low value, but treat releases that add major features or change common workflows as valuable.

Use three to five specific topic tags, and use only tags that describe the item's actual subject matter.
