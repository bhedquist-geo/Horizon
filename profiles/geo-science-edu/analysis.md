# Evaluation goal

Evaluate how useful this item is to a university professor of geography and geoscience who teaches introductory earth science, regional geography, and GIS courses, conducts research on climate, water resources, and remote sensing, and looks for funding. Score the item itself, not the reputation of its source. Score 0 for any item that is not written in English.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Exceptional.** An important, well-evidenced finding, resource, or opportunity, such as a major result in climate or geoscience that could be brought into a class right away, or a well-matched funding opportunity with clear eligibility and deadlines.
- **7-8: High value.** Credible, well-supported research or reporting with clear significance, a teaching idea with concrete details, or a relevant funding or policy announcement.
- **5-6: Useful.** On topic and accurate but incremental, narrow, or of limited relevance to teaching or research.
- **3-4: Marginal.** Loosely related, repeats well-known points, opinion without evidence, or thin coverage.
- **0-2: Noise.** Off topic, promotional, sensationalized, too incomplete to evaluate, or not in English.

# Short items

Many items from news and funding feeds arrive as only a headline and a one or two sentence snippet. Do not penalize such an item for lacking depth, methods, or evidence. Judge it on the significance and relevance of what it reports. A clearly relevant short item can score 7 or higher. Examples include a funding opportunity, program, or policy change in geoscience, geospatial, climate, or education research that names an agency or program, a news report of a notable climate, water, or geoscience result or event, and a concrete example of AI or GIS being used in teaching. Score a short item 0-4 only when it is off topic, generic, promotional, or too vague to say what happened.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward peer-reviewed or otherwise well-documented evidence, clear significance, honest treatment of uncertainty, and data or visuals that would work in teaching. For funding items, reward clear eligibility, amounts, and deadlines when they are stated, and give more weight to opportunities with nearby deadlines. Do not reward sensationalism, advocacy without evidence, or press releases that repackage a study without added context.

Use three to five specific topic tags, and use only tags that describe the item's actual subject matter.
