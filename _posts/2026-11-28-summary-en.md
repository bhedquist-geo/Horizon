### [Sinking African Coastal Megacities Face Severe Flood Risks by 2050](https://www.carbonbrief.org/sinking-african-megacities-will-expose-seven-million-people-to-flooding-by-2050) ⭐️ 8.0/10

New research published in Science Advances combines more than 4,000 satellite images with sea level projections to evaluate flood risks across 20 populous coastal African cities. The study finds that land subsidence—driven by human activities like groundwater pumping—combined with rising seas will expose over seven million people, one million buildings, and $180bn in assets to extreme flooding by mid-century. Lagos and Alexandria are identified as primary hotspots, sinking at rates of 5.2mm and 6.3mm per year, respectively.

rss · Carbon Brief · Nov 27, 19:00

**「Context」** The analysis builds on IPCC sea level rise scenarios and historical data gaps regarding vertical land movement in African coastal regions. Subsidence refers to the sinking of the ground caused by human activities or tectonic processes, which exacerbates relative sea level rise. The authors note important limitations: the flood model assumes no coastal defenses and holds population and city data constant at 2020 levels, representing a worst-case physical picture that does not account for future urban migration or adaptation.

**「Relevance」** This study provides valuable material for teaching remote sensing applications, GIS spatial analysis, and urban vulnerability in the Global South. Researchers can use the demonstrated satellite-based methodology to assess vertical land motion in other data-scarce coastal regions.
