### [Sinking African Coastal Megacities Face Severe Flood Risks by 2050](https://www.carbonbrief.org/sinking-african-megacities-will-expose-seven-million-people-to-flooding-by-2050) ⭐️ 8.0/10

New research published in Science Advances reports that sinking land combined with rising sea levels will expose more than seven million people in 20 African coastal megacities to flooding by 2050. Using over 4,000 satellite images from Sentinel-1 alongside IPCC sea level projections, the study identifies Lagos and Alexandria as subsidence hotspots sinking at rates of 5.2mm and 6.3mm per year, respectively. Under moderate warming, extreme sea level events could expose one million buildings and $180bn in assets across the assessed cities.

rss · Carbon Brief · Nov 27, 19:00

**「Context」** The study isolates the physical impact of vertical land movement—ground subsidence driven by human activities like groundwater pumping—and sea level rise by holding population and city footprints constant at 2020 levels. The authors note that the flood model acts as a worst-case physical picture because it assumes no coastal defenses and does not account for future urban growth or population migration. Satellites provided vital remote sensing data to monitor land movement in previously data-scarce coastal regions of the global south.

**「Relevance」** This research offers valuable material for teaching urban geography, satellite remote sensing applications, and natural hazards in the global south. Researchers can also build upon the methodology to evaluate coastal vulnerability in other data-scarce regions.

**Tags**: `#remote sensing`, `#sea level rise`, `#subsidence`, `#urban geography`, `#flooding`
